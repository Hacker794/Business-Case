# Riverside Malls Smart Trolley Pilot — Results

## Recommendation

Riverside Malls should launch the 25-trolley pilot at a starting price of **£200 per trolley per month**.

This is the lowest tested price that produces a positive monthly profit and allows the pilot to break even with fewer than 25 trolleys.

## Results

| Price per trolley | Contribution per trolley | Monthly profit | Break-even trolleys |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| £150 | £110 | -£250 | 27 |
| £200 | £160 | £1,000 | 19 |
| £250 | £210 | £2,250 | 14 |
| £300 | £260 | £3,500 | 12 |

## What the results show

At **£150 per trolley**, the pilot would lose £250 each month. It would need approximately 27 trolleys to break even, which is more than the 25 trolleys included in the pilot. This means £150 is not a financially viable price.

At **£200 per trolley**, the pilot would make a monthly profit of £1,000. It would break even at approximately 19 trolleys, so a 25-trolley pilot would cover its costs with some margin.

At **£250 per trolley**, the monthly profit would rise to £2,250 and the pilot would break even at approximately 14 trolleys.

At **£300 per trolley**, the monthly profit would be £3,500 and the pilot would break even at approximately 12 trolleys.

## Why recommend £200 rather than £250 or £300?

The £250 and £300 options produce more profit, but the model does not include evidence about how much customers are willing to pay.

A higher price may reduce demand or make it harder to attract operators to the pilot. £200 is therefore the safer starting price because it is profitable while being the lowest of the three required price tiers.

## Important limitation

The model assumes all 25 trolleys are paid for throughout the month. It does not yet include possible customer churn, unused trolleys, higher support costs or unexpected maintenance costs.

These risks should be tested through a sensitivity analysis before making a full rollout decision.

## Final conclusion

The numbers support launching the pilot at **£200 per trolley per month**.

The £150 price should be rejected because it does not break even. The £250 and £300 prices could be tested later if Riverside Malls finds that customers are willing to pay more.
