# Riverside Malls Smart Trolley Pilot — Case Framing

## Situation

Riverside Malls is considering launching a paid pilot of 25 smart trolleys. The trolleys are intended to improve the shopping experience while creating a new source of revenue.

## Complication

The technology may be useful, but the pilot only makes business sense if the monthly revenue covers the fixed and variable costs. Riverside Malls also needs to choose a price that is profitable without making the service too expensive for customers.

## Question

Should Riverside Malls launch the 25-trolley paid pilot, and what monthly price should it charge per trolley?

## Initial Hypothesis

Riverside Malls should launch the 25-trolley pilot at £200 per trolley per month because this price is expected to cover the costs and produce a positive monthly profit while remaining lower than the £250 and £300 options.

## Numbers That Matter

- Number of trolleys: 25
- Variable cost per trolley: £40 per month
- Fixed costs: £3,000 per month
- Price tiers to test: £200, £250 and £300 per trolley per month
- Additional floor price for comparison: £150 per trolley per month

## Key Calculations

Monthly profit:

Monthly profit = number of trolleys × (price per trolley − variable cost) − fixed cost

Break-even number of trolleys:

Break-even trolleys = fixed cost ÷ (price per trolley − variable cost)

## Decision Criteria

The pilot should only be recommended if:

- The 25 trolleys generate a positive monthly profit.
- The break-even number of trolleys is below 25.
- The chosen price gives Riverside Malls some protection if costs increase.
- The price is still realistic enough for customers to accept.

## What Will Be Tested Next

The next step is to calculate the monthly profit and break-even point at each price tier. The results will show whether the initial £200 recommendation is supported by the numbers.
